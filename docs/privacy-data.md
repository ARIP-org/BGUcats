# What the app keeps about a person

For App Store Connect's "App Privacy" answers (owner step O16) and for
the privacy policy (O10). **Do not edit this file:** it is the output of
`scripts/privacy_table.py`, where every line has its reason, and
`tests/test_privacy_table.py` fails if the two differ or if the code
writes a key the script does not classify.

Apple's words are from its App Privacy Details page as read on
2026-11-14. "Collected" means sent off the phone and kept; "linked"
means tied to the person's account.

## The answers

Every kind below is **linked to the person** and used for **App
Functionality** only.

| Apple's group | Kind | Why the app has it | Where it is kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact Info | **Email Address** | The Google account a person signs in with: who may sign in, and whose account it is. Never shown to another member. | `ADMIN`, `ENTITY`, `IDENTITY`, `PROFILE`, `SESSION`, The sign-in service (Cognito), The servers' logs (CloudWatch) |
| Contact Info | **Name** | The Google account's name, which sign-in receives, and the display name a person chooses for others to see. | `PROFILE`, The sign-in service (Cognito) |
| Location | **Precise Location** | Where a cat was met: the phone's position when a photo is taken, rounded to three decimal places (about 100 m) before it is stored. Apple calls three decimal places or more precise. A photo that arrives as a file (the phone's own camera, or the library) is stored as it is, with whatever position it carries. | `PHOTO`, `SESSION`, The media bucket, `raw/` and `derived/` |
| Contacts | **Contacts** | Who a person's friends are in the app, and whom they blocked. Apple's kind includes a social graph. The phone's address book is not read. | `BLOCK`, `BLOCKEDBY`, `FREQ`, `FRIEND` |
| User Content | **Photos or Videos** | The photos of cats a person takes, which are also the dataset the recognition model is measured on, and their profile photo. | `PHOTO`, `POST`, `PROFILE`, The media bucket, `raw/` and `derived/`, The media bucket, `avatars/` |
| User Content | **Other User Content** | What a person writes or chooses: posts, name ideas and votes, likes, feedings, new-cat proposals, reports, and which cat they said they met. | `BY`, `ENC`, `FED`, `FEDCAT`, `LIKE`, `NAME`, `PICK`, `POST`, `PROPOSAL`, `REPORT`, `VOTE` |
| Identifiers | **User ID** | The account's id, the username, and the sign-in service's id for the Google account. | `AUTHOR`, `IDENTITY`, `INVITE`, `PROFILE`, `PROPOSER`, `SUBMITTER`, `USER`, `USERNAME`, The sign-in service (Cognito), The servers' logs (CloudWatch) |
| Identifiers | **Device ID** | The phone's address for Apple's notifications and the id the app gives the phone. Only once notifications are turned on (N2). | `DEVICE` |
| Usage Data | **Other Usage Data** | What the app counts from a person's activity: cats met, points, badges, and short-lived counters that limit how often an action can be repeated. | `BADGE`, `MET`, `METM`, `PUSHED`, `RATE`, `STATS` |
| Diagnostics | **Other Diagnostic Data** | The servers' logs, which name account ids, and for an admin's actions email addresses. | The servers' logs (CloudWatch) |

## To know before answering

- **Tracking: no.** Nothing is shared with a data broker or joined with anyone else's data for advertising. There is no advertising SDK and no analytics SDK in the app.
- **Who else holds it.** Amazon Web Services stores and runs everything, as a processor. Google is asked who a person is at sign-in and is sent nothing else. When moderation is built (C1) Google will also be sent what is about to become public; this file changes with it.
- **Deleting.** `DELETE /api/v1/me` (B10a) deletes everything the app's own table holds about the person: profile, username, invite code, friends, requests, blocks, likes, votes, feedings, counters, badges, devices and the profile photo; their posts are removed and no longer name them, names they suggested and reports they made stay with them cut out. Since B10b the person is signed out of every device and their sign-in (the Cognito user) is deleted first, before anything in the table; a token already handed out works for up to an hour after, and is answered as deleted while the job runs. In the dataset, the photos, encounters and answers to "Who did you meet?" they sent stay (D14), and since #135 their email address is taken off them, and off the list of who may sign in unless they are an admin. What still links them: a hash of the address, not salted, on those encounters and photos. There is no button for it in the app yet (the Swift app's profile). Apple requires one before the App Store.
- **Not from the iPhone app.** The Telegram bot's contributors are in the same tables (`SUBMITTER#`, `TGMSG#`) under a salted hash, and the landing page counts visits with no visitor in them. Neither is the app's collection.

## Outside the two tables

| Where | Kinds | What |
|---|---|---|
| The media bucket, `raw/` and `derived/` | Photos or Videos, Precise Location | Every photo as it arrived, and smaller copies with their EXIF removed. An original that arrived as a file keeps its own EXIF, position included. |
| The media bucket, `avatars/` | Photos or Videos | Profile photos, made small on the phone, with no EXIF. |
| The sign-in service (Cognito) | Email Address, Name, User ID | The Google account's email, name, picture address and id, as Google gives them at sign-in. |
| The servers' logs (CloudWatch) | Other Diagnostic Data, User ID, Email Address | Account ids on most writes; email addresses where an admin adds or removes a person. No retention is set: they are kept until someone deletes them. |
